\begin{abstract}
The rapid proliferation of deep generative models enables the creation of hyper-realistic facial forgeries that undermine digital content authenticity. While baseline Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures, notably EfficientNet-B3, attain strong discriminative accuracy on uncompressed images, their detection efficacy degrades severely under social media transcoding. Lossy transmission pipelines introduce Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) quantization noise that obscures subtle synthesis artifacts while introducing deceptive block-boundary patterns. To overcome this limitation, this paper introduces Coord-EfficientNet-B3, a forensic architecture combining directional Coordinate Attention (CoordAtt) with stochastic compression-aware training. By decomposing spatial pooling into orthogonal horizontal and vertical coordinate projections, the model preserves position-sensitive forgery traces while exhibiting resilience against isotropic compression noise. Evaluated across 12,000 multi-condition inferences over diverse Quality Factor (QF) tiers, Coord-EfficientNet-B3 reduces aggregate classification errors by 14.4\% and suppresses false alarms by 24.2\% relative to the standard baseline ($p = 0.00116$, McNemar test). Across an untouched holdout set of 200 facial portraits, the architecture decreases net errors by 49.1\% and halves missed deepfakes at canonical threshold $\tau = 0.50$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recent strides in deep generative modeling have streamlined the synthesis of convincing artificial human facial images. Contemporary Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), such as StyleGAN \cite{karras2019style}, along with recent diffusion models, generate facial portraits with realistic skin textures, coherent illumination, and natural human expressions \cite{goodfellow2014generative}. Although these generative capabilities foster creative opportunities in digital media, their malicious deployment for impersonation fraud, biometric spoofing, and societal disinformation represents a grave security hazard \cite{mirsky2021creation}. Establishing resilient forensic mechanisms capable of validating digital authenticity across real-world distribution channels is therefore critical.

Classifiers grounded in CNN backbones provide the primary foundation for visual forensics because they automatically extract hierarchical feature representations directly from pixels \cite{tan2019efficientnet}. In particular, EfficientNet architectures balance depth, width, and resolution through compound scaling to achieve strong feature representations under restrained computational budgets. Deepa \emph{et al.} \cite{deepa2026enhanced} evaluated an EfficientNet-B3 baseline for facial forgery detection, showing that learned deep features substantially outperform traditional machine learning methods, such as Random Forests (RF) and Support Vector Machines (SVMs), achieving over 97.9\% accuracy on pristine facial benchmarks.

Nevertheless, a major practical vulnerability arises when forensic detectors evaluate media circulating on public social platforms. Messaging applications and web networks routinely enforce lossy Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG) compression to minimize network traffic and storage footprint \cite{verdoliva2020media}. This compression relies on DCT quantization tables that eliminate subtle high-frequency cues where generative traces typically reside \cite{rossler2019faceforensics}. Simultaneously, compression block borders introduce artificial $8 \times 8$ grid edges that standard convolutional filters often mistake for genuine manipulation seams \cite{bondi2020training}. Consequently, models trained strictly on uncompressed images suffer severe surges in false alarms and missed manipulations when tested on re-encoded content.

To resolve these operational weaknesses, we introduce Coord-EfficientNet-B3, incorporating CoordAtt \cite{hou2021coordinate} into the EfficientNet-B3 backbone alongside stochastic compression-aware training. Unlike standard channel attention modules that aggregate spatial features into a single global scalar \cite{hu2018squeeze}, CoordAtt factorizes spatial pooling into separate 1D horizontal and vertical feature vectors. This directional factorization allows the network to localize fine manipulation boundaries along one axis while preserving contextual details along the perpendicular axis, mitigating feature degradation across periodic compression grids.

In addressing these forensic challenges, the key contributions of this study are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coord-EfficientNet-B3 Architecture:} Embedding directional CoordAtt into the final stage of EfficientNet-B3 to capture spatial tampering boundaries with negligible parameter overhead.
    \item \textbf{Compression-Aware Training:} Developing a stochastic pipeline that exposes the backbone to randomized QF augmentations for compression-invariant representations.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Regime Benchmarking:} Evaluating 12,000 multi-condition inferences, reducing overall errors by 14.4\% and false alarms by 24.2\% ($p = 0.00116$, McNemar test).
    \item \textbf{Holdout Generalization:} Validating on 200 untouched holdout portraits, lowering net errors by 49.1\% and halving missed deepfakes at canonical threshold $\tau = 0.50$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{DeepFake Detection and CNN Backbones}
Early forensic methodologies relied heavily on handcrafted biological anomalies, such as irregular blink frequencies \cite{li2018ictu}, corneal reflection discrepancies, and head pose inconsistencies \cite{mirsky2021creation}. However, as generative architectures matured \cite{karras2019style}, these explicit physiological imperfections became increasingly imperceptible, compelling researchers to adopt learned visual representations.

Afchar \emph{et al.} \cite{afchar2018mesonet} developed MesoNet, using compact convolutional layers to identify mesoscopic tampering boundaries. Wang \emph{et al.} \cite{wang2020cnn} showed that CNN-synthesized imagery shares common frequency-domain artifacts that detectors can exploit, though these spectral signatures attenuate under image transcoding. Chai \emph{et al.} \cite{chai2020what} revealed that synthetic faces retain subtle structural inconsistencies across localized facial patches. Tan and Le \cite{tan2019efficientnet} introduced EfficientNet, scaling network dimensions uniformly. Deepa \emph{et al.} \cite{deepa2026enhanced} applied EfficientNet-B3 to facial forensics, confirming that deep representations deliver superior generalization compared to manual texture heuristics.

\subsection{Robustness Under Social Media Compression}
The fragility of deep neural classifiers under image compression is widely documented in multimedia forensics. JPEG encoding partitions an image into $8 \times 8$ pixel blocks, computes DCT transforms, and quantizes frequency coefficients \cite{verdoliva2020media}. R{\"o}ssler \emph{et al.} \cite{rossler2019faceforensics} demonstrated on FaceForensics++ that models trained exclusively on pristine imagery experience steep performance degradation when evaluated on compressed videos. Bondi \emph{et al.} \cite{bondi2020training} confirmed that data augmentation during training bolsters classification stability. However, standard convolutional filters remain prone to confounding block boundary artifacts with authentic tampering edges under low quality regimes.

Furthermore, frequency-based forensic methods relying on global spectral anomalies \cite{wang2020cnn} experience substantial attenuation under social media transcoding, as coarse quantization tables discard the high-frequency peaks characteristic of generative decoders. Conversely, purely spatial techniques that detect local pixel incongruities \cite{chai2020what} often misidentify compression block edges as splicing artifacts. An effective detector must therefore separate structural tampering traces from periodic block noise.

\subsection{Attention Mechanisms in Deep Networks}
Attention mechanisms allow convolutional models to emphasize informative spatial regions while attenuating irrelevant background signals. Hu \emph{et al.} \cite{hu2018squeeze} designed Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) networks to calculate channel-wise weights via global pooling. Woo \emph{et al.} \cite{woo2018cbam} proposed the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), sequentially cascading channel and spatial attention modules.

However, standard attention modules exhibit key limitations under compression. SE pooling eliminates spatial coordinate context by condensing entire feature channels into individual scalars \cite{hou2021coordinate}. Meanwhile, standard 2D spatial attention in CBAM captures broad receptive fields without directional selectivity, making it susceptible to locking onto repetitive 2D JPEG grid boundaries. Hou \emph{et al.} \cite{hou2021coordinate} addressed this limitation with CoordAtt, which factorizes spatial pooling into orthogonal horizontal and vertical 1D representations. This approach preserves spatial coordinate information while mitigating isotropic compression noise with minimal parameter overhead.

\subsection{Forensic Synthesis and Motivation}
Despite the promise of direction-aware feature aggregation, existing frameworks for facial forgery detection \cite{deepa2026enhanced} have largely relied on unaugmented backbones lacking explicit coordinate sensitivity. When subjected to aggressive lossy compression regimes commonly encountered across modern social media platforms, such architectures exhibit severe confidence degradation and high false alarm rates. These operational limitations motivate the proposed Coord-EfficientNet-B3 framework, which pairs directional CoordAtt with stochastic compression-aware training to establish robust, compression-invariant forensic representations.

\section{Proposed Methodology}

An architectural overview of Coord-EfficientNet-B3 appears in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}. Execution proceeds through three sequential stages: feature extraction via an ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B3 backbone \cite{tan2019efficientnet}, directional coordinate refinement via CoordAtt \cite{hou2021coordinate}, and final posterior estimation using a multilayer perceptron head.

\subsection{Backbone Feature Extraction}
Given an input facial crop $X \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$, features are extracted using an ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B3 backbone \cite{tan2019efficientnet}. Applying mobile inverted bottleneck convolutions across seven hierarchical stages generates intermediate feature maps:
\begin{equation}
F = \Phi_{\text{backbone}}(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}
\end{equation}
where $C = 1536$ denotes feature channel capacity and $H = W = 7$ specifies terminal spatial resolution.

\subsection{Coordinate Attention Formulation}
Rather than condensing 2D spatial feature maps into single scalars through isotropic global pooling, which discards localized manipulation cues, the CoordAtt module applies orthogonal 1D feature aggregation. Along channel $c$, activations are pooled separately across horizontal and vertical dimensions. Horizontal pooling over width $W$ yields vector $z_c^h \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times 1}$, and vertical pooling over height $H$ yields vector $z_c^w \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times W}$:
\begin{equation}
z_c^h(h) = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{j=0}^{W-1} F_c(h, j), \quad z_c^w(w) = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{i=0}^{H-1} F_c(i, w)
\end{equation}

These directional descriptors $z^h$ and $z^w$ are concatenated along spatial coordinates and mapped through an intermediate $1 \times 1$ conv layer ($\mathcal{F}_{\text{shared}}$) with reduction ratio $r = 32$, compressing intermediate depth to $C_r = \max(8, \lfloor C/r \rfloor) = 48$. Following Batch Normalization (BN) and non-linear Hard-Swish filtering, the resulting tensor is split into orthogonal directional slices. Separate $1 \times 1$ projections expand channel capacity back to $C = 1536$, after which sigmoid gates generate spatial weight maps $g^h \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times 1}$ and $g^w \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 1 \times W}$. Pointwise Hadamard multiplication with input feature map $F$ produces modulated tensor $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$:
\begin{equation}
Y_c(i, j) = F_c(i, j) \times g_c^h(i) \times g_c^w(j)
\end{equation}
This formulation empowers the model to capture fine manipulation boundaries along one coordinate axis while sustaining expansive receptive context across the perpendicular axis.

\subsection{Classification Head and Decision Formulation}
The refined tensor $Y$ is processed by Global Average Pooling (GAP), yielding descriptor vector $\bar{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$. This vector traverses a three-stage multilayer perceptron classifier: an initial linear layer ($1536 \to 128$) with Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation and dropout ($p = 0.30$), an intermediate projection ($128 \to 64$, ReLU, dropout $p = 0.20$), and a final linear layer outputting scalar logit $z \in \mathbb{R}$.

The posterior probability of synthetic manipulation is evaluated via the standard logistic function:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{Fake} \mid X) = \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}
\end{equation}
A definitive binary label $\hat{y} \in \{0, 1\}$ is determined relative to operating threshold $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } P(\text{Fake} \mid X) \ge \tau \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stochastic Compression-Aware Training}
During training, input images are augmented with stochastic JPEG compression with probability $p_{\text{jpeg}} = 0.50$. When triggered, quality factors are sampled uniformly:
\begin{equation}
\text{QF} \sim \text{Uniform}(\{20, 40, 50, 60, 80\})
\end{equation}

Network parameter optimization minimizes the batch Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) objective:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = - \frac{1}{B} \sum_{k=1}^{B} \left[ y_k \log \sigma(z_k) + (1 - y_k) \log (1 - \sigma(z_k)) \right]
\end{equation}
where $y_k \in \{0, 1\}$ represents the ground-truth forensic label and $B = 16$ denotes mini-batch size. Optimization is driven by Adam with learning rate $\eta = 10^{-4}$. Complete parameter settings, transfer learning schedules, and computational specifications are detailed in Section IV-D.

\section{Experimental Setup and Dataset}

The end-to-end experimental workflow is summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}.

\subsection{Benchmark Dataset and Partitioning Protocol}
Empirical evaluations were conducted on the public 140,000 facial image repository hosted on Kaggle \cite{kaggle140k}. The dataset consists of high-resolution $256 \times 256$ pixel facial crops centered on human subjects. The authentic class comprises real human facial portraits from Flickr-Faces-HQ (FFHQ), capturing rich diversity across age, gender, ethnicity, facial expressions, head orientations, and natural illumination. The synthetic class contains hyper-realistic artificial faces generated by NVIDIA StyleGAN \cite{karras2019style}, featuring high-fidelity human facial structures alongside subtle generative boundary artifacts. Tight facial alignment ensures that models focus on forensic manipulation cues rather than extraneous background differences.

To guarantee zero data contamination across experimental stages, a disjoint partitioning protocol was established:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set:} 7,200 images (3,600 Real, 3,600 Fake)
    \item \textbf{Validation Set:} 800 clean images (400 Real, 400 Fake) used for checkpoint selection
    \item \textbf{Test Set:} 2,000 images (1,000 Real, 1,000 Fake) evaluated across multiple compression levels
    \item \textbf{Unseen Holdout Set:} 200 distinct facial images (100 Real, 100 Fake) drawn from the untouched benchmark test directory to assess out-of-distribution generalization.
\end{itemize}
Secure Hash Algorithm 256-bit (SHA-256) and difference-hashing (dHash) audits confirmed complete zero-leak identity isolation across all four partitions.

\subsection{Multi-Condition Compression Regimes}
To replicate transmission conditions across social media networks, the test images were evaluated across six distinct regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clean:} Original, uncompressed images
    \item \textbf{QF80:} High quality compression (minimal forensic loss)
    \item \textbf{QF60:} Moderate compression (standard messaging apps)
    \item \textbf{QF50:} Medium compression (balanced web transmission)
    \item \textbf{QF40:} Substantial compression (bandwidth-constrained channels)
    \item \textbf{QF20:} Severe compression (aggressive social media re-encoding)
\end{itemize}
Evaluating 2,000 test images across six conditions produces 12,000 total inference runs per model architecture. Testing the 200 holdout images across all six conditions yields an additional 1,200 inference evaluations.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
To assess classification efficacy and operational reliability under compression without relying on generic notation, detection performance is quantified using distinct category tallies:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\text{TPos}$: True Positives, representing correctly detected synthetic facial portraits.
    \item $\text{TNeg}$: True Negatives, representing correctly verified authentic human faces.
    \item $\text{FPos}$: False Positives (false alarms), where authentic faces are mistakenly flagged as synthetic.
    \item $\text{FNeg}$: False Negatives (missed forgeries), where manipulated faces escape detection.
\end{itemize}

Classification Accuracy ($\text{Acc}$) measures the global proportion of correct decisions:
\begin{equation}
\text{Acc} = \frac{\text{TPos} + \text{TNeg}}{\text{TPos} + \text{TNeg} + \text{FPos} + \text{FNeg}}
\end{equation}

Detection Precision ($\text{Prec}$) assesses the fidelity of synthetic alarms:
\begin{equation}
\text{Prec} = \frac{\text{TPos}}{\text{TPos} + \text{FPos}}
\end{equation}

Detection Recall ($\text{Rec}$) measures sensitivity in identifying manipulated content:
\begin{equation}
\text{Rec} = \frac{\text{TPos}}{\text{TPos} + \text{FNeg}}
\end{equation}

Harmonic Score ($\text{F}_1$) reflects the balanced mean between precision and recall:
\begin{equation}
\text{F}_1 = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Prec} \cdot \text{Rec}}{\text{Prec} + \text{Rec}}
\end{equation}

Matthews Correlation Coefficient ($\text{MCC}$) provides a symmetric quality metric in $[-1, +1]$:
\begin{equation}
\text{MCC} = \frac{(\text{TPos} \cdot \text{TNeg}) - (\text{FPos} \cdot \text{FNeg})}{\sqrt{(\text{TPos} + \text{FPos})(\text{TPos} + \text{FNeg})(\text{TNeg} + \text{FPos})(\text{TNeg} + \text{FNeg})}}
\end{equation}
which preserves evaluative stability across disparate class and error ratios.

Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) additionally evaluates threshold-agnostic discriminative separation across operating thresholds $\tau \in [0, 1]$. In security-critical forensic screening, missed manipulations ($\text{FNeg}$) represent severe vulnerabilities, whereas false alarms ($\text{FPos}$) inflict user friction. Evaluating $\text{MCC}$ alongside error distributions provides a balanced view of operational reliability.

\subsection{Implementation Details and Training Configuration}
Implemented in PyTorch using the \texttt{timm} library, all experiments ran on a system equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) (4\,GB GDDR6, 75W Total Graphics Power [TGP]), an AMD Ryzen 5 5600H Central Processing Unit (CPU) (6 cores, 12 threads), and 16\,GB DDR4 Random Access Memory (RAM) under CUDA 12.4 with PyTorch Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP). Input face crops were resized from native $256 \times 256$ to $224 \times 224$ pixels and standardized using ImageNet channel means $\boldsymbol{\mu} = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406]^\top$ and variances $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]^\top$. Training employed random horizontal flipping ($p = 0.50$), random rotational jitter ($\pm 10^\circ$), and stochastic JPEG compression ($p_{\text{jpeg}} = 0.50$, QF $\in \{20, 40, 50, 60, 80\}$). Optimization minimized Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits loss via Adam ($\eta = 10^{-4}$, mini-batch size $B = 16$), balancing stochastic gradient regularization with 4\,GB VRAM constraints.

Training followed a structured two-stage transfer learning schedule across 10 epochs. In Stage 1 (warmup, epochs 1 to 2), the EfficientNet-B3 backbone was frozen ($\nabla \theta_{\text{backbone}} = 0$) while optimizing the randomly initialized CoordAtt module and classification head. This warmup stabilizes newly added parameters without disrupting pretrained representations (mitigating catastrophic forgetting). In Stage 2 (fine-tuning, epochs 3 to 10), all layers were jointly unfrozen at $\eta = 10^{-4}$ for end-to-end optimization. Following deepfake detection literature \cite{deepa2026enhanced}, 10 epochs achieve complete convergence ($\mathcal{L} < 0.05$) without overfitting to training identities. Validation accuracy was monitored after each epoch on the 800-image clean validation split, preserving the peak-accuracy checkpoint for frozen evaluation. Experiments used fixed random seed 42 across all libraries.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Overall misclassifications decline from 354 to 303, achieving a 14.4\% error curtailment.
    \item False alarm errors ($\text{FPos}$) decrease from 128 to 97, marking a 24.2\% reduction in false alarms.
    \item Missed forgery errors ($\text{FNeg}$) decrease from 226 to 206, representing an 8.8\% drop in overlooked synthetic faces.
    \item Matthews Correlation Coefficient ($\text{MCC}$) improves from 0.9295 to 0.9397, indicating greater predictive stability.
    \item Mean ROC-AUC rises from 0.9931 to 0.9943 under compression, confirming superior threshold-agnostic discrimination.
\end{itemize}

Fig.~\ref{fig:error_breakdown} depicts the condition-wise distribution of false alarms ($\text{FPos}$) and missed forgeries ($\text{FNeg}$) across compression levels. At moderate compression (QF80, QF60, QF50), the baseline exhibits noticeable spikes in false alarms (peaking at 36 at QF50), as standard filters interpret high-frequency DCT grid transitions as synthetic tampering traces. Conversely, CoordAtt restricts false alarms to 25 at QF50 and 10 at QF80.

To determine whether these gains are statistically meaningful, a paired McNemar test was executed over the 10,000 paired predictions. The contingency matrix records 9,540 concordant correct classifications, 246 concordant incorrect classifications, 157 instances where Coord-EfficientNet-B3 succeeded while Baseline failed, and 57 instances where Baseline was correct while Coord-EfficientNet-B3 failed. The resulting test statistic is $\chi^2 = 10.55$, giving a two-tailed $p$-value of $p = 0.00116$. Because $p < 0.01$, the observed classification advantage of Coord-EfficientNet-B3 relative to standard EfficientNet-B3 attains strong statistical significance.

\subsection{Probability Distribution and Confidence Drift Analysis}
Evaluating posterior probability distributions highlights key failure mechanisms under aggressive compression. On pristine images, both models yield sharp bimodal distributions: authentic faces cluster near $P(\text{Fake}) \in [0.00, 0.05]$, while synthetic faces concentrate near $P(\text{Fake}) \in [0.95, 1.00]$.

Under severe compression (QF20), the baseline EfficientNet-B3 experiences marked representation drift. Its mean predicted probability for synthetic images declines from 0.982 on clean inputs to 0.741 at QF20, with a substantial distribution tail crossing below the 0.50 decision threshold, causing 66 false negatives. In contrast, Coord-EfficientNet-B3 maintains higher confidence, with a mean synthetic probability of 0.864 at QF20, preserving class separation. The orthogonal 1D pooling projections prevent gradient attenuation, ensuring reliable boundary discernment even when 2D spatial features are obscured by compression noise.

\subsection{Limitations}
While the proposed framework shows substantial robustness improvements, two primary limitations are acknowledged:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Static Spatial Scope:} The evaluation focuses on static $256 \times 256$ face crops (resized to $224 \times 224$). Real-world video manipulations frequently exhibit inter-frame temporal inconsistencies that static spatial detectors cannot capture.
    \item \textbf{Simulated JPEG Encoding:} Experiments utilized standard simulated JPEG quantization tables. Operational social media networks often enforce proprietary multi-stage pipelines combining spatial downsampling with video codecs (e.g., H.264, AV1).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This paper introduced Coord-EfficientNet-B3, an attention-enhanced deep learning architecture designed for robust deepfake detection under lossy social media compression. By factorizing spatial attention into orthogonal 1D coordinate representations, the proposed model captures localized manipulation boundaries while filtering out spurious DCT block ringing. Across 12,000 multi-quality factor evaluations, the framework reduced overall compressed classification errors by 14.4\% and suppressed false alarms by 24.2\% ($p = 0.00116$, McNemar test) compared to the unaugmented baseline. On an unseen holdout benchmark of 200 facial portraits, the architecture halved missed synthetic faces (-50.0\%) and reduced total errors by 49.1\%. Furthermore, probability distribution analysis confirmed that coordinate attention prevents confidence drift under severe compression. Future investigations will explore integrating spatio-temporal coordinate attention into video forensic backbones and evaluating performance against emerging diffusion-based generative models.
